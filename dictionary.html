Anatomical Integrity

Anatomy & Form

Figures must have realistic physical proportions. Elements must match correctly; a giant should not stand next to a tiny house unless the depth justifies it.

Braccio Measurement

Anatomy & Form

Alberti used the height of an average human body (divided into three braccia, or Florentine cubits) as the core scale module for everything else in the painting. One Florentine cubit is approximately equal to 58.36 cm.

Chiaroscuro (Light & Dark)

Light & Value

Shadows must match the trajectory of a singular, defined light source to make forms appear solid and three-dimensional.

Circumscription

Perspective & Space

This step establishes the structural framework of the scene by drawing the outer contours and outlines of all objects.

Composition

Composition & Notan

This step dictates how the individual shapes, lines, and figures are proportionally linked together to form an organized narrative.

Eyeline

Perspective & Space

The direction in which a person or actor looks, representing their line of sight or gaze. The whole system of perspective drawing is based on the height of the eyeline; whether or not the eye line is above or below the subject.

Grace and Variety

Composition & Notan

A good composition must show movement, emotive variety, and balanced arrangements of figures to tell a compelling story.

Horizon Line

Perspective & Space

The apparent line where the earth, land, or sea seems to meet the sky. The horizontal line that is passing directly through this vanishing point to set the viewer's eye height.

One Point Perspective

Perspective & Space

A drawing method that uses a single vanishing point on the horizon line to create the illusion of three-dimensional depth on a flat, two-dimensional surface.

  • The Convergence Rule: Any set of parallel lines that are not parallel to your picture (drawing) plane will appear to slant toward and meet at a single vanishing point.
  • The Horizon Line Rule: The horizon line always sits at the exact eye level of the viewer. Vanishing points for horizontal ground-plane or ceiling-plane lines must lie directly on this horizon line.
  • True Parallel Exception (No Convergence): Lines that are physically parallel and parallel to your flat piece of paper or picture plane do not converge. They stay genuinely parallel and straight (for example, the front face of a building looking at you straight-on).
  • Vertical Lines: In standard 1-point and 2-point perspective, all vertical lines (heights) remain strictly vertical (perpendicular to the ground) and do not slant toward a vanishing point. (Vertical lines only gain a vanishing point in 3-point perspective when you look sharply up or down at a tall object.)

Orthogonal Lines

Perspective & Space

Two lines that intersect or cross each other at a right angle (90 degrees). Lines connecting the base boundaries of the canvas to this centric point. These guide lines create the illusion of recession into deep space.

Perspective

Perspective & Space

An art technique used to make a subject have dimensions, look solid, appear close up, appear far away, or to suggest a feeling of space. The system was mathematically formalized around 1415 in Florence, Italy, by architect Filippo Brunelleschi and formalized by Leon Battista Alberti.

Picture Plane

Perspective & Space

The flat, two-dimensional surface of a canvas, paper, or screen, or an imaginary transparent window placed between the viewer's eye and the subject.

Reception of Light

Light & Value

The final stage covers color, light, and shadow to give flat shapes realistic volume.

The Four True Colors

Light & Value

He recognized four primary colors associated with the natural elements: Red (Fire), Blue (Air), Green (Water), and Ash/Grey (Earth).

Three Sets of Parallel Lines

Perspective & Space

Refer to three distinct groups of straight, coplanar lines (lines that lie on the same flat surface) where every line in a given set runs alongside the others in that same set without ever crossing (i.e., a brick's Length line, Width line, and Height line). In a 1-point perspective drawing, any two lines representing receding parallel lines (such as the top left and top right length lines of a brick) can be used to find the vanishing point by tracing and extending them forward until they cross each other. A horizontal line drawn through this point gives us the eye-level. Key properties include:

  • No Intersection: Lines within the same set never meet.
  • Constant Distance: The perpendicular distance between any two lines in the same set is always equal.
  • Same Slope: On a coordinate plane, all lines belonging to the exact same parallel set share the same mathematical slope y = mx + b.
  • Notation: Lines are typically marked with arrows (like > or >>) to show which lines belong to which parallel set.

Transversal Line

Perspective & Space

A straight line that crosses two or more other lines at different, distinct points in the same plane. To accurately shrink objects as they recede, create a proportional floor grid (tessellation).

Vanishing Point

Perspective & Space

The location on a horizon line in a perspective drawing or photograph where receding parallel lines appear to meet and converge. This is a single point inside the drawing canvas that aligns directly with the eye level of the ideal viewer.

White and Black

Light & Value

Alberti taught that white and black are not true colors, but modifiers used to create light and shadow.
