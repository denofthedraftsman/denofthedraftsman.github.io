Origin & Mission

About Den of the Draftsman

The story behind committing to a zero-compromise, traditional foundation in drawing, spatial geometry, and technical illustration.

Starting from Ground Zero

Many aspiring artists jump straight into complex digital software, shortcut plugins, or loose painterly rendering without ever learning how to place a three-dimensional box reliably into space.

Den of the Draftsman was founded on the opposite premise: to go from absolute zero experience to advanced technical proficiency by returning to the primary source materials of classical draftsmanship.

By working through a deliberate 6-phase sequence of 22 foundational textbooks—spanning classical perspective (Ernest R. Norling), sight-size contour drawing (Harold Speed), light physics (Anson K. Cross), pencil handling (Arthur L. Guptill), constructive anatomy (George Bridgman, Andrew Loomis), and industrial design markers (Scott Robertson, Koos Eissen, Ronald Kemnitzer)—every skill is practiced openly and verified by physical scans.

Physical Tools of the Trade

Analog Drafting Instruments
  • Surfaces: 100% rag vellum, 2-ply Strathmore 500 series Bristol board, and bleed-proof marker layout bond.
  • Linework: Hardwood drafting boards, steel T-squares, adjustable triangles, mechanical clutch pencils (4H to 4B), and technical fineliners (0.05 to 0.8mm).
  • Permanent Tones: Waterproof India ink on vellum, feather-touch alcohol dye markers, and gouache specular accents.

No AI generation is used in any study plate, drawing, or definition on this website. Everything cataloged here is created, scanned, and authored by hand as I work through the curriculum.

Learning in Public

Instead of locking the study process away until a pristine commercial portfolio exists, I stream the drafting board sessions live. You can watch exercises being sighted, perspective grids being calculated, and ink lines being pulled in real time.
