Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions regarding the zero-to-mastery path, curriculum sequencing, analog materials, stream interactions, and commission inquiries.

Why start with traditional drafting instead of digital software?

Philosophy

Digital software offers undo buttons, symmetry rulers, transform handles, and layers that make it easy to mask fundamental weaknesses. Traditional drafting on physical vellum and Bristol board offers zero auto-correction: if a perspective line misses its vanishing point, or if an ink stroke wobbles, it is immediately obvious.

The Principle Mastering physical tools forces deep muscle memory, decisive linework, spatial visualization, and an unshakeable grasp of three-dimensional form that transfers seamlessly to any medium later.

Can someone really go from zero experience to advanced artist this way?

The Journey

Yes. Classical draftsmanship is not an unteachable talent; it is an applied visual science governed by geometry, optics, anatomy, and physics. By breaking the path into 6 discrete phases—starting with basic box rotations and sight-size measurement before ever attempting complex figure work or industrial rendering—every skill builds logically on the preceding one.

Verification Every exercise sheet and study plate is scanned and published openly in the Selected Works archive so the entire learning curve is completely transparent.

Is any of the artwork, study plates, or definitions on this site AI-generated?

Authenticity

Absolutely not. Every drawing plate, ink study, and sketchbook page displayed on this website is hand-drawn using analog drafting instruments on physical paper and scanned. All definitions in the Draftsman's Lexicon are read, researched, and compiled directly from the 22 physical textbooks in our curriculum.

What core starter materials do I need to follow along?

Equipment

To follow Phase 1 (Norling and Speed), you do not need expensive gear:

  • • A 2mm leadholder with 2H (layout lines) and HB/2B lead (contours).
  • • A standard 24-inch or 30-inch drafting T-square and 30°/60° acrylic triangle.
  • • Strathmore 400 or 500 series Bristol paper (11x14 in.) or smooth drawing paper.
  • • A basic mechanical compass and a drafting eraser (kneaded and plastic block).

How long is each phase expected to take?

Pacing

There is no arbitrary rush. Progression to the next book is based on demonstrated competence rather than a calendar deadline. Typically, completing all exercise plates for a book like Norling's Perspective Made Easy takes 4 to 8 weeks of consistent daily practice. The entire 22-book curriculum is designed as a deep, multi-year self-taught atelier course.
